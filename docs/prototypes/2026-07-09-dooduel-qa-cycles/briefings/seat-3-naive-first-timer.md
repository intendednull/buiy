# Seat supplement — Naive First-Timer

> ORCHESTRATOR: this seat gets **only** `COMMON.md` + this file. Do **NOT** show it
> the other seat supplements, the QA charter, the reference design, or
> `known-issues.md`. Its whole value is a genuinely fresh, uncoached pair of eyes —
> any of those documents would spoil it. (Charter §4.)

You have **never played this game before**. There is no study guide. Play it exactly
the way a real first-time player would: figure it out as you go from what you see on
`screen.png` and read in `ui.md`, using the clicking/typing/drawing mechanics in
`COMMON.md`.

Your **main deliverable is the free-text confusion log** — the `## Session report`
in your `report.md`, especially **"How it felt"**. It matters more than any YAML
finding. As you play, write down, in the moment:

- **Every time you didn't know what to do next** — "I'm on this screen… what now?"
- **Every affordance you had to guess at** — a button whose purpose wasn't obvious,
  where the room code was, how to start, how to pick a word, how to draw, how to
  guess, how to move on after a round.
- **How long each screen took you to understand**, and where you got stuck.
- **Anything that surprised, delighted, confused, or annoyed you.**

Rules that keep your signal honest:

- **Do NOT self-censor, and do NOT look for a "known issues" list** (you weren't
  given one on purpose). Report **every** confusion freely — even if it turns out to
  be already known, your fresh-eyes confusion is still data. The orchestrator triages
  and dedupes for you.
- **Still play honestly.** Take your turns; when it's your turn to draw, make a real
  attempt at drawing the word (zero strokes = a failed turn — `COMMON.md` §4);
  guess other players' drawings. Follow the **loop discipline** in `COMMON.md` §4 so
  you don't go idle and miss your turns.
- Keep the YAML findings **light** — if something is clearly broken (a crash, a dead
  button), jot a quick one, but spend your effort narrating the *experience*.

That's your whole brief. Go play, and tell us what it was like.
